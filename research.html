Research

Aging is a loss of flexibility.

Our central hypothesis is that aging and age-related chronic disease stem from a progressive loss of metabolic flexibility, the ability of cells, tissues and whole organisms to respond acutely and appropriately to changes in nutrient supply. Our mission is to define the mechanisms that maintain this flexibility, and to target them to promote healthy aging.

Our framework

Homeodynamics, not homeostasis

Aging is often described as a loss of homeostasis, a drift away from a fixed set point. That view has quietly shaped the field: it suggests that switching some process permanently on or off should recreate youth.

We think about aging differently. A young system is homeodynamic: it swings widely and recovers quickly as conditions change, from fed to fasted, active to resting, stressed to calm. With age that dynamic range narrows. The aim isn’t to lock a system in one state. It is to restore its ability to respond.

01

How cells sense nutrients and energy

Two conserved kinases, AMPK and mTORC1, read a cell’s energy and nutrient state and decide how it responds. We study how they work together to keep metabolism flexible, and how that coordination fails with age.

Will’s postdoctoral work showed that AMPK extends lifespan through the CRTC/CREB transcriptional switch. In our lab, we found that this signal acts from neurons to reprogram metabolism and longevity across the whole body. We also showed that inhibiting mTORC1 in neurons alone can extend life without the costs to growth and reproduction that usually accompany it. Today we are mapping how nutrient cues reach mTORC1 through the Rheb–GATOR1 machinery, and how AMPK and mTORC1 together mediate the benefits of dietary restriction and intermittent fasting.

  • Lifespan extension induced by AMPK and calcineurin is mediated by CRTC-1 and CREB Nature, 2011
  • Neuronal CRTC-1 governs systemic mitochondrial metabolism and lifespan via a catecholamine signal Cell, 2015
  • Neuronal TORC1 modulates longevity via AMPK and cell nonautonomous regulation of mitochondrial dynamics eLife, 2019
  • AMPK at the nexus of energetics and aging Cell Metabolism, 2014
02

Organelle remodeling and metabolic flexibility

Switching between fuels takes physical change. Mitochondria fuse and fragment, peroxisomes multiply, and organelles talk to one another. We study this remodeling as the cellular hardware of metabolic flexibility.

We found that dietary restriction and AMPK extend lifespan only if mitochondrial networks stay dynamic and peroxisomes can respond alongside them. ER-to-mitochondria calcium signaling links stress responses to longevity, and mitochondria form nutrient-responsive derived structures. Most recently we described a peroxisome-led inter-organelle cascade that orchestrates metabolic flexibility and longevity. We are now asking how this remodeling is lost with age.

Key papers

  • Peroxisomes orchestrate metabolic flexibility and longevity via an interorganelle cascade Nature Aging, 2026
  • Dietary restriction and AMPK increase lifespan via mitochondrial network and peroxisome remodeling Cell Metabolism, 2017
  • Atf-6 regulates lifespan through ER-mitochondrial calcium homeostasis Cell Reports, 2020
  • Causal roles of mitochondrial dynamics in longevity and healthy aging EMBO Reports, 2019
03

RNA splicing, lipids and the epigenome

Flexibility is also written in how genes are read. We were the first to show that RNA splicing factors causally regulate aging.

Splicing factor 1 is required for dietary restriction and reduced TORC1 signaling to extend life, and the early-life activity of splicing factors determines how well longevity interventions work later on. We have also found that chromatin modifiers act through CRTC-1, and, with the Brunet lab, that lipid droplets and peroxisomes are co-regulated to extend lifespan. Together these results connect gene regulation to lipid metabolism and long-term health.

Key papers

  • Splicing factor 1 modulates dietary restriction and TORC1 pathway longevity Nature, 2017
  • The efficacy of longevity interventions is determined by the early life activity of RNA splicing factors PLoS Biology, 2025
  • The CRTC-1 transcriptional domain is required for COMPASS complex-mediated longevity Nature Aging, 2023
  • Lipid droplets and peroxisomes are co-regulated to drive lifespan extension Nature Cell Biology, 2023
04

Toward precision geroscience

Interventions such as dietary restriction slow aging in almost every organism, but not equally in every individual. We want to know who will benefit, and why.

We are building biomarkers that predict an individual’s response to pro-longevity interventions. One example is endogenous mitochondrial NAD(P)H, which can forecast lifespan. With collaborators, we are testing whether age-related changes in redox signaling are regulated rather than random, mapping how dietary restriction reshapes them in mice. We are also asking whether keeping neurons metabolically flexible protects against Alzheimer’s disease. Through the Harvard Chan Healthy Aging Initiative, we connect this biology to measures of biological age in people and to equity in who gets to age well.

Key papers

  • Endogenous mitochondrial NAD(P)H fluorescence can predict lifespan Communications Biology, 2024
  • Redefining age-based screening and diagnostic guidelines: an opportunity for biological age Lancet Healthy Longevity, 2022
  • Do you want to live forever? Lessons learned from the biology of aging PLoS Biology, 2026
  • Metabolic communication and healthy aging: where should we focus our energy? Developmental Cell, 2020
Our approach

The system that answers the question

Aging mechanisms are conserved from single cells to people, so we choose each model for what it can reveal and then test what we find across systems.

Cells and mice

Mammalian cell biology, metabolism and physiology, including dietary restriction and fasting in mice, to test conserved mechanisms in mammals.

People

Measures of biological age and metabolic flexibility in human cohorts, through collaborations and the Harvard Chan Healthy Aging Initiative.

Short-lived models

Genetic tools in C. elegans let us test cause and effect across an entire lifespan in weeks, and we share these tools openly with the field.
