Our question

Why does growing old make us sick? Age is the biggest risk factor for almost every chronic disease. We want to know why, and whether it has to be that way.

Most chronic diseases, including neurodegenerative disorders, cardiovascular disease, stroke, cancer, metabolic disease and osteoporosis, become more common with age. Yet eating less or fasting slows aging and delays these diseases in nearly every organism studied, while obesity speeds them up.

The common thread is metabolism. A young body switches easily between fuels, from fed to fasted, and recovers quickly. We call this capacity metabolic flexibility, and we think its loss drives aging. Our goal is to find the mechanisms behind it and restore it, capturing the benefits of dietary restriction without its costs.

The big idea

Homeodynamics, not homeostasis

Aging is usually described as a loss of homeostasis, a drift away from a fixed set point. We see it as a loss of dynamic range. Young systems respond widely and recover quickly as conditions change. Old systems respond less, and more slowly.

So the goal isn’t to lock biology into one “youthful” state. It is to restore the capacity to respond.

What we study

Keeping metabolism flexible as we age

We study how cells and organisms sense nutrients and switch metabolic state, why that flexibility is lost with age, and how to restore it. We use whatever system best answers the question, from cells and mice to people and short-lived model organisms.
